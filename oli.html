Section C6: four sentences, a box of eight look-alike words, 8 marks. Every word in the box has a twin that sounds almost the same. Train your ear and eye on the one letter that changes the meaning.

Paper 2 sections and marks, as set in the 2026 prelims (90 marks). C6 is the only section where the trap is a single letter.
Mastered

The highest-frequency word pairs, most important first. Match each meaning to its spelling before you meet the pair in a sentence.

Core pairs mastered

How the drill trains the brain

  • Retrieval, not rereading. You pull the word out of memory before you see the answer, which strengthens recall more than reviewing a list.
  • Contrast pairs. Both twins appear side by side with the changed letter highlighted, so the brain stores the difference instead of blurring the two.
  • Spacing. A missed word returns three cards later, and words you know well come back less often.
  • Confidence check. A lucky guess is not counted as mastered. This keeps false confidence out of the exam hall.
